01About Me

A computer is a
software developer's sketchbook.

I’m an undergraduate Computer Science student at Florida International University with a strong interest in software development and problem solving. I like working through the entire process of a project, from understanding the problem and planning the solution to building, testing, fixing, and improving it.

I’m especially interested in work where technology has a practical purpose. I want what I build to make something easier, clearer, faster, or more useful for the person on the other side of it.

I moved to the United States from Nicaragua when I was eight years old, and a lot of what has driven me since then has been the goal of building a better future for both myself and my family. That has meant taking opportunities wherever I could find them and learning from very different kinds of work. I’ve worked as a student office assistant, supported student organizations and administrative operations, spent years working as a barista, completed a systems engineering internship where I worked with Linux servers and internal web applications, and now work as a die maker in manufacturing. Each position has taught me something different about responsibility, communication, problem solving, and showing up consistently.

A lot of my technical growth has also happened outside of the classroom. Building TrainRec, developing a pill identification application, and creating a website for JCBlinds have each forced me to solve different kinds of problems. Those projects taught me that writing the code is only part of the work. How something looks, how a user moves through it, how errors are handled, and whether the final product actually solves the original problem matter just as much.

My path has not been completely linear, but I think that is part of what has shaped how I approach problems. I have had to learn in classrooms, offices, coffee shops, manufacturing environments, and through projects I built on my own. Most of what I know has come from being willing to start somewhere, figure things out, and keep working until I have something better than what I started with.
